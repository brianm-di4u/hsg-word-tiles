# Arcades and Amusements: Coin-Op, Slot Machines and Pinball in Hot Springs and Arkansas

This file covers coin-operated amusement and gambling machines in Hot Springs and Arkansas: the 1930s court cases that treated marble and pinball machines as gambling devices, the 1933/1939 free-replay statute, the 1966 payoff ruling, today's bingo-pinball ban, the slot machines of the illegal-casino era (Spa Amusement Co., the Mills slot at the Old State House), and the 1967 raids and destruction of seized machines. It also records what was **not** found: no documented pinball parlor or penny arcade on Central Avenue in any era.
Everything here was added on 2026-11-01 from web research gathered for the Spa City Pinball game (`games/Pinball/research/web-findings.md`, all sources accessed 2026-11-01); see `SOURCES.md` → "Game-session research merged 2026-11-01". Fuller club, figure and raid history is in `gangster-era-and-gambling.md`; Whittington Park's early-1900s amusements are in `other-attractions.md`.
Confidence: no tag = full read of the linked source; `(snippet)` = search-summary only; `conflict:` / **[DISPUTED]** = sources disagree; **[UNVERIFIED]** = not supported by a reliable source; **[inferred]** = design inference, not fact.

Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

## Arkansas law on pinball and coin-op machines

- **1930, the "mint vender" case.** In *Rankin v. Mills Novelty Co.* (Oct. 27, 1930, from Pulaski County) the Arkansas Supreme Court held that a Mills Novelty "Baseball Mint Vender" was a gambling device. A nickel bought mints plus a chance number of slugs to play a baseball game, and the court counted free play as a "thing of value" — [CAP: 182 Ark. 561](https://cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools/ark/182/cases/0561-01.json). `roaring`
- **1934, marble machines per se.** *Steed v. State*, 189 Ark. 389 (June 11, 1934): each nickel released 10 marbles that the player shot with levers onto a numbered board, and the high score won a prize. The court held these marble machines gambling devices per se, even when sometimes run "for amusement only," and affirmed an order to destroy machines seized from a Pine Bluff cigar store and sandwich shop — [CAP: 189 Ark. 389](https://cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools/ark/189/cases/0389-01.json). `roaring`
- **1937, "Home Run."** *Stanley v. State*, 194 Ark. 483 (July 12, 1937) applied *Steed* to a 5-cent electric baseball marble machine in a Little Rock barber shop — [case.law](https://cite.case.law/ark/194/483/) (snippet). `roaring`
- **1933/1939, free replays allowed.** The state never banned flipper pinball outright. Act 137 of 1933, as amended by Act 201 of 1939 (Ark. Stat. § 84-2611), legalized the free-game feature — cited in *Bostic* (below).
- **1966, payoffs are the line.** *Bostic v. City of Little Rock*, 241 Ark. 671 (Dec. 19, 1966): free games alone are lawful, but a pinball machine at a Little Rock cafe whose free games were paid off in cash (a nickel a game) became a gaming device — [CAP: 241 Ark. 671](https://cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools/ark/241/cases/0671-01.json). `midcentury`
- **Today.** Ark. Code § 5-66-111 outlaws multi-coin "bingo-type" pinball machines that add odds or extra free games; its stated intent is to prohibit "so-called 'bingo'-type pinball machines," and it cites the federal shipping ban in 15 U.S.C. § 1172 — [FindLaw: Ark. Code § 5-66-111](https://codes.findlaw.com/ar/title-5-criminal-offenses/ar-code-sect-5-66-111/). A 2023 Attorney General opinion (No. 2023-008) still uses the *Steed* (per se) vs *Bostic* (illegal by use) split — [ezel.ai copy](https://ezel.ai/ag-opinions/ar/2023-0008-q1-does-arkansas-code-annotated-26-57-401-exempt-amusement-d) (snippet; secondary host).
- **Reading [inferred]:** in the 1930s Arkansas courts treated marble and pinball machines as gambling devices; from 1933/1939 the law allowed free-replay pinball, and enforcement targeted cash payoffs. No statewide or Hot Springs ban on pinball itself was found (unlike New York City, below). Slot machines were a separate matter: illegal throughout, but openly run in Hot Springs until 1967 (`gangster-era-and-gambling.md`).
- **National context.** New York City banned pinball in 1942 and lifted the ban in April 1976; Chicago, Los Angeles, Boston, New Orleans and Philadelphia also had big-city bans — [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/that-time-america-outlawed-pinball) (secondary, snippet).
- **False lead, do not use.** A 1981 *News-Record* story about a pinball and video gameroom ordinance in "Hot Springs" is from Madison County, **North Carolina** (Hot Springs, NC), not Arkansas — [DigitalNC, Nov. 26, 1981](https://newspapers.digitalnc.org/lccn/sn92074087/1981-11-26/ed-1/seq-1/).

## Slot machines in the illegal-casino era

- **Spa Amusement Co.** supplied slot machines to the Vapors, the Belvedere Country Club and other Hot Springs casinos. Dane Harris was an owner and Ernie Bone ran it. It closed for a few months in 1964 and reopened in March 1965 — [ADG 2017 "The slot machine man"](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2017/oct/07/the-slot-machine-man-20171007/) (full read 2026-11-01; the corpus previously cited it via search summary). `midcentury`
- **What the machines were like.** Hot Springs slots "featured five ways to hit a jackpot" and came in nickel, dime and quarter versions (ADG, citing Threadgill's book); some carried stickers reading "this machine made by Spa Amusement" — same.
- conflict: EOA calls the company "Spa City Amusement Co." and says Tony Frazier saw an FBI raid on it in 1967; ADG calls it "Spa Amusement" — [EOA Gambling Museum](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/history-of-hot-springs-gambling-museum-15477/); [ADG 2017](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2017/oct/07/the-slot-machine-man-20171007/).
- **Whether Spa Amusement also handled pinball or jukeboxes was not found.**
- **The Old State House slot.** The Old State House Museum (Little Rock) holds a Hot Springs slot machine: a Mills Novelty Co. (Chicago) 5-cent machine with a 1910 design copyright, serial 338046, catalogued c. 1950 (object 2009.117.06). Each nickel paid out a piece of gum plus a play credit, a ploy to pass as a vending machine. It was confiscated from an unnamed Hot Springs gambling hall — [Old State House Museum collections](https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/20244/slot-machine-from-hot-springs-arkansas). This is the only specific slot model tied to Hot Springs in the corpus.
- **Raids before 1967.** On Feb. 29, 1956, State Police raided two Hot Springs clubs, arrested 17 people and seized gambling equipment from the Southern Club and the Pine Supper Club — [EOA "This Day"](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=15745). The Jan. 4, 1963 bombing of the Vapors exposed "slot machines and craps tables" to the street — [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/) (history card only; never gameplay).

## The 1967 raids and the destruction of machines

- **Aug. 17, 1967:** State Police director Lynn Davis brought 20 troopers and seized equipment from the Bridge Street, Citizens, Ohio and White Front clubs. None were operating; the operators had been tipped off — [ADG 2017](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2017/oct/07/the-slot-machine-man-20171007/). (EOA and Wikipedia say "mid-August"; this gives the day.)
- **October 1967:** troopers took 111 slots from Southern Club manager Harry Columbus's home on Bellaire Drive. The same day they raided a slot repair shop on Westbrook Street, and "more than 100" machines were taken across the two raids — same.
- **Destruction:** the seized machines were taken to a rock quarry, run over by a bulldozer, then burned (no date or quarry name given) — same. An Old State House Museum photo caption (object 2008.085.03) says Col. Davis and the State Police "hauled the evidence to a gravel pit for its destruction" after the October 1967 raids seized "hundreds of slot machines" — [Old State House photo 2008.085.03](https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/16347/photograph-arkansas-state-police--hot-springs-gambling-rai). A second photo (2008.085.02) shows Capt. Bill Miller, Maj. Melven DeLong and Sgt. Jim Tyler "destroying gambling equipment" from a 1967 Hot Springs raid — [Old State House photo 2008.085.02](https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/16346/photograph-arkansas-state-police--burning-gambling-items). EOA: "gambling devices were destroyed in large numbers," which broke the illegal trade — [EoA: Southern Club](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/southern-club-1208/).
- conflict: "more than 100" (ADG, two October raids) vs "hundreds of slot machines" (Old State House caption). Keep both; say "over a hundred."
- **[UNVERIFIED] "public burning."** A popular blog says the machines were burned **publicly**. The primary sources place the destruction at a quarry or gravel pit and do not describe a public event — [All Around Arkansas blog (2022)](https://allaroundarkansas.blogspot.com/2022/06/all-around-arkansas-gambling-and-spa.html). Do not show a public bonfire as history.
- **The Vapors is not documented as raided in 1967.** The sources list the four clubs above, Columbus's home and the Westbrook Street shop. Game text must say "the clubs," not "the Vapors was raided."

## Hot Springs-themed pinball and arcade titles

- **"Hot Springs," D. Gottlieb & Co., 1937.** A single-player electromechanical pinball (KLOV/MOG #11772; IPSND dates it 5/1937); it appears in the January 1940 *Coin Machine Journal* list of 1931–39 games — [arcade-museum.com: Hot Springs](https://arcade-museum.com/Pinball/hot-springs). Existence documented; **theme unknown**. It came out the same year as Gottlieb's horse-race titles (*Daily Races*, *Multiple Races*), so it may have a racing or payout theme, but that is unconfirmed. No backglass description or surviving examples are listed.
  - **Do-not-use note:** do not title a game just "Hot Springs," do not imitate this machine's name, and avoid a 1937-style horse-race or one-ball payout layout as a table's core identity. "Spa City Pinball" does not collide with it.
- No Bally, Williams, Stern, Mills or video-arcade title themed on Hot Springs, Arkansas was found (absence of evidence). Hot Springs, **South Dakota** also used "Carlsbad of America" and is a different place.

## Game hooks

All **[inferred]** design ideas; mechanics are original game systems, not historical simulation.
1. **Free-replay match and replay knocker (Spa City Pinball).** A "replay" credit is period-correct for Arkansas pinball after 1933/1939; never show cash payouts, which *Bostic* (1966) made illegal. Source: Arkansas law section.
2. **Raid! 1967 hurry-up.** Hidden "slot machines" light on the bank; collecting them evokes the October 1967 seizures. Text: "Troopers raid the clubs" / "Over 100 slots seized." Never "the Vapors was raided." Source: 1967 section.
3. **Gravel-pit epilogue.** After the raid, a short DMD or card animation of machines being crushed at a gravel pit (not a public bonfire). Source: 1967 section.
4. **Slot-reel mystery award.** Five-way-jackpot flavor and nickel/dime/quarter tiers, with **original** reel art (no Mills or other real machine artwork). The gum-vend disguise is a good history card. Source: Slot machines section.
5. **Marble-machine prequel / attract card.** "1934: Arkansas courts call marble machines gambling devices" as an attract-mode fact. Source: law section.
6. **Trivia (Valley of Vapors Trivia Trail).** *Steed* (1934), the 1933 free-game act, the 111 slots of October 1967, the Old State House Mills slot.
7. **Bathhouse Barons / Through the Ages.** A "slot route" underground property supplied by a fictional amusement company (Spa Amusement archetype); a 1956 raid event card; a 1967 "machines destroyed" card. Source: `gangster-era-and-gambling.md` hooks.

## Caveats

- **Legal reading:** the "no pinball ban" conclusion is inferred from four Arkansas Supreme Court cases and the statute citations inside *Bostic*; no city ordinance search for Hot Springs was completed. The 1933 and 1939 acts were not read directly, only as cited.
- **Case hosts:** three opinions were read on a Caselaw Access Project mirror (`cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools`) and one via `cite.case.law` (snippet). Confirm citations against an official reporter before printing them as quotes.
- **AG Opinion 2023-008** was seen only on a secondary host (snippet).
- **Name conflict:** Spa Amusement Co. (ADG) vs Spa City Amusement Co. (EOA).
- **Count conflicts:** October 1967 machines "more than 100" (ADG) vs "hundreds" (Old State House caption); Gambling Museum slots 65 (ADG 2017) vs 80 functional, 15 used in Hot Springs (EOA) — keep both as dated values.
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** the "public burning" of slot machines (blog only); a historic Central Avenue arcade (none found); Spa Amusement handling pinball (not found); the theme of Gottlieb's 1937 "Hot Springs" (unknown).
- **Do not use:** the 1981 Hot Springs, **NC** gameroom ordinance; Hot Springs, SD material.
- **Sensitive history:** the 1959 Belvedere and 1963 Vapors bombings are history cards only, never scored or played for laughs. Real people (Davis, Columbus, Harris, Bone, the troopers) appear by name only in fact text, never as characters or likenesses.
- **No logos or brand marks:** no Mills, Gottlieb, Bally or other manufacturer art or trade dress; reel symbols and cabinets are original designs.
