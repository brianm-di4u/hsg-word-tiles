# Bathhouse Row and the Springs: Buildings, Lost Bathhouses, Promenades and Bathing Practice

Eight bathhouses built 1892–1923 stand on the east side of Central Avenue between Reserve and Fountain streets, mostly by the Little Rock firm Mann & Stern, on federal land that could never be bought, only leased. Only the Buckstaff has run baths continuously since 1912; the others closed 1962–1985 and were re-leased 1989–2019 as a visitor center, spa, brewery, hotel, cultural center and park store, while the Maurice (vacant since 1974) began a $31.6M restoration in 2025. This file gives per-building facts, visual descriptions with estimated hex swatches, the lost and segregated bathhouses, the promenades, the springs and fountains on the Row, and bath prices by year.
Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

## Row overview

**District facts**
- Bathhouse Row is on Central Ave. between Reserve and Fountain Streets, about 6 acres, at 34°30′49″N 93°3′13″W. Listed on the NRHP Nov 13, 1974 (ref. 74000275), designated a National Historic Landmark District May 28, 1987, and put on the National Trust's "11 Most Endangered" list in 2003 (removed May 2007). — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row)
- NRHP point for the district: 34.513889, -93.053611. — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas)
- The Row plus the Grand Promenade became a National Historic Landmark District in 1987 — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/). The cultural landscape became an NHL on May 28, 1987 — [NPS Bathhouse Row Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm); [Wikipedia, Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row)
- Called the largest remaining collection of twentieth-century bathhouses in the US. — [NPS Bathhouse Row Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm)
- Three periods of significance: 1885–1897, 1912–1923 and 1930–1959. An "informal Victorian landscape design" dates to the 1890s. — [NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm)
- Bathhouses are "made of masonry and steel." The 1912–1923 replacements were "fire-resistant brick and stucco bathhouses, several of which featured marble walls, billiard rooms, gymnasiums, and stained glass windows." — [NPS, Bathhouse Row](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/bathhouse-row.htm)
- The Row is federal reservation land; the 1877 act set boundaries, sold excess lots, taxed thermal water and appointed a superintendent, so Row lots were leased, not sold — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/); [EOA Rector v. US](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/).

**Build sequence** `gilded-spa` → `roaring`
- 1873: six bathhouses and 24 hotels/boardinghouses near the springs — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- 1880–1888: larger Victorian bathhouses (the last in 1888). 1882–1883: Hot Springs Creek enclosed in an underground arch (conflict: some NPS summaries say 1882–84; designed for a 10-year flood — [NPS Hot Springs Creek (via search summary)](https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-creek.htm)). 1890s: Army Capt. (Robert R.) Stevens oversaw landscaping. By 1901 all springs walled and covered. 1912–1923: brick and stucco replaced wood — [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/); [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm).
- Construction dates of the surviving row: Hale 1892–93 (oldest survivor), Maurice 1911–12 (George Gleim Jr.), Buckstaff 1912, Fordyce 1914–15 (Mann & Stern), Superior 1916 (Harvey C. Schwebke), Quapaw 1922, Ozark 1922 (Mann & Stern), Lamar 1923 — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/). The Lamar's 1923 opening completed Bathhouse Row — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm).
- NPS opening years and architects: Lamar 1923 (Roberts & Schwebke); Buckstaff 1912; Ozark 1922 (Mann & Stern); Quapaw 1922 (Mann & Stern); Fordyce 1915 (Mann & Stern); Maurice 1912 (George R. Gleim Jr.); Hale 1892; Superior 1916 (Harry C. Schwebke). — [NPS, Bathhouse Row](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/bathhouse-row.htm)
- Mann & Stern designed or remodeled at least five Row buildings plus the 1924 Arlington **[inferred]** (count from the sourced per-building notes).
- Other architects from the NPS per-bathhouse sheets and the CLI: the Hale's original architects were **George and Fremont Orff**, and the Buckstaff's was **Frank W. Gibb and Company** — [NPS Hale sheet](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/bathhouses/hale.pdf); [NPS Buckstaff sheet](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/bathhouses/buckstaff.pdf); [CLI](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/cli-bathhouse-row.pdf). The Lamar is credited to Harry Schwebke (NPS Lamar sheet, CLI) or Roberts & Schwebke (NPS Bathhouse Row page) — **[DISPUTED]**.

(northernmost)

| Completed 1916 `gilded-spa` | Harry C. - **Features:** green tile medallions; pilasters with imitation tile; marble stalls and stairs; cooling tank added 1920; it replaced a Victorian Superior of the 1880s — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). Concrete painted to imitate ornamental tile, with green tile medallions and paterae above brick pilasters that carry patterned insets; flat roofs behind brick parapets; marble stair treads and tile wainscoting — [Wikipedia, Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). Superior "had green neon script and relied on its reputation for service" — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/).
- **History:** Damaged, though not severely, in the May 1923 flood — [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/). Closed 1983 with furnishings auctioned (another section of the same article says 1984) — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). "The Superior operated as a bathhouse until it closed in 1984" — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). The brewery has operated since 2012 — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Brewery:** described as the only brewery in a U.S. national park and the only one brewing with 100% thermal spring water — [Arkansas Tourism](https://www.arkansas.com/hot-springs/landmarks/superior-bathhouse-brewery); [VinePair](https://vinepair.com/articles/arkansas-superior-bathhouse-beer-national-park/). **[DISPUTED]** soft claim: the thermal-water "only" claim rests partly on owner-brewer Rose Schweikhart's belief — [All About Beer](https://allaboutbeer.com/superior-bathhouse-brewery/). NPS pipes water to the brewery at about 143–144°F, saving energy because brewing starts by heating water to 160°F; Schweikhart notes most hot spring water elsewhere is not potable (sulfur, bacteria, algae) — [All About Beer](https://allaboutbeer.com/superior-bathhouse-brewery/).
- **Visual description:** flat parapet with brick pilasters; painted faux-tile concrete; green tile medallion/paterae frieze. Swatches **[inferred]** est.: cream brick #E6D7B5, white stucco #F3EFE6, Maurice/Superior green tile #4E7D5B, marble #F2EFE8 with veins #B9B4AA. Paterae rosette motif in green #4E7D5B for Superior (visual_style motif 9). Neon script green (EOA; hex not sourced).
- **Anecdotes:** none sourced.

- **Visual description:** cream first story; the top two stories light-yellow brick with a dark-brown lozenge (diamond) band; two low red-tile hipped end pavilions with a flat centre and a set-back skylight penthouse; arched stained-glass-and-copper marquee. ## Promenades, entrance and landscape

- **Main entrance and balustrade:** designed in the 1890s by Army engineer Robert Stevens, with two bronze federal eagles and a central hemicycle drinking fountain. A bandstand on the balustrade was removed in 1958 — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). A 2010 view shows "symmetrical hedges and columns" at the Main Entrance — [NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm).
- **Magnolia Promenade:** laid out by Robert Stevens in the 1890s with double rows of magnolias; now a single row — [Wikipedia, Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). A 1940s caption describes a "tree-lined walkway" — [NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm). On the HABS site plan the single row of magnolias stands along the curb about **25 ft apart** [measured] — [HABS AR-28](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/habs-bathhouse-row.pdf); `games/mahjong/research/bathhouse-row-facades.md`. The 1890s plan had a 14-ft concrete promenade and a 9-ft lawn border; by 1939 it was "a 12 foot wide concrete walkway"; today a curbed turf strip of Southern Magnolias, the walk, a low Chinese-holly hedge and each building's lawn — [CLI](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/cli-bathhouse-row.pdf).
- **Jens Jensen:** in 1916 Stephen Mather brought Jens Jensen in to add lights and flower gardens — [Wikipedia, Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Lamp posts:** the 1984 HABS photos show **dark green cast-iron posts, each with five white globe lamps** (one on top, four on arms), along the Central Ave walk and at the Main Entrance; HABS AR-28-I-6 records a "typical light standard" **[observed 1984]** — [HABS AR-28-I](https://www.loc.gov/item/ar0040/); [HABS 570630c](https://www.loc.gov/resource/hhh.ar0035.color.570630c). Whether these posts date from Jensen's 1916 lighting is unknown.
- **Grand Promenade:** north–south brick path on the hillside behind the bathhouses, Reserve Ave. to Fountain St.; replaced a "meandering Victorian path" with a more formal line; gravel by WWII; paving brick replaced 1984 — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row). Dates conflict: construction began 1930s and finished early 1960s (Wikipedia; the [NHL nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/74000275_text): "completed in the early 1960s"); grading began 1933 and completed 1958 ([NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm)); final landscaping July 25, 1958 ([NPS Grand Promenade sheet](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/bathhouses/grand-promenade.pdf)); "built 1930s–1958" ([NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm)). Width 22 ft as graded, 18 ft from 1956 ([CLI](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/cli-bathhouse-row.pdf)); named a National Recreation Trail April 13, 1982 (NPS GP sheet); OSM length about 2,390 ft (0.45 mi) including the Fountain St footway (see `bathhouse-row-layout.md`). Trail data: 0.5 mi, 3.7% grade, 619–717 ft, a National Recreation Trail and the entry point from downtown — [NPS trails page](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/hot-springs-and-north-mountain-trails.htm).
- **Hot Springs Creek arch:** creek rises near Whittington Avenue, runs in a tunnel under Bathhouse Row (Central Ave.), surfaces south of the Row and empties into Lake Hamilton — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, AR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). OSM traces a **culvert about 6,100 ft long** (6,118 ft) from under Park Ave through the Park/Whittington "Y", then south **under the east edge of Central Ave / the Magnolia Promenade**, 10–15 m east of the centerline, past Reserve St, to daylight at 34.507257, -93.052004 [derived] — [OSM way/1213972844](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1213972844); `games/trivia/research/bathhouse-row-layout.md`. The bathhouses were built on its east bank ([NHL](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/74000275_text)). Where it rises is an open question (Wikipedia: near Whittington Ave; OSM: up Park Ave). Arch date conflict: 1882–83 (NPS History) vs 1884 (NHL) vs completed 1886 ([CLI](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/cli-bathhouse-row.pdf)).
- **Floods on the Row:** May 1923 (Superior damaged) — [EOA BR](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/); May 1990 (nearly 13 in.; bathhouse basements filled to the ceiling; thermal pumps and reservoirs contaminated, so thermal water to fountains and bathhouses shut off) — [NPS Morning Report](https://npshistory.com/morningreport/incidents/hosp.htm); May 19–20, 2020 (two flood waves flooded Bathhouse Row; about 85 rescued) — [KARK (via search summary)](https://digital-release.kark.com/?p=352188). In 1913 the Central Avenue bathhouses were saved by a late wind shift — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/).

## African American bathing history

- Bath attendants (mentioned from 1875) were mostly African American until the 1980s — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- 1860: Hot Spring County had 613 enslaved people out of 5,635 residents; many worked in hotels, and one hotel advertised baths for enslaved people, who were then returned to their owners `frontier` — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- 1880s: Black patrons could buy tickets at the Ozark, Independent and possibly Rammelsberg bathhouses, but not during the 10 a.m.–noon hours doctors considered best — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- 1890: A.C. Page ran the Independent as a Black bathhouse for less than a year; William G. Maurice bought it and reopened it for whites only — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- 1898: Free Bathhouse remodeled on a segregated plan; 1921–22 rebuilt with four separate sections — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- 1904: Crystal Bathhouse (415 Malvern Ave., John McCaslin), first built exclusively for African Americans; Knights of Pythias lease 1908; burned 1913 — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- Dec. 27, 1914: Pythian Bathhouse and Sanitarium (architect W.T. Bailey of Tuskegee) opened with half-price baths for Pythian insurance members; enlarged 1923; closed 1974 — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [LOC HABS (via search)](https://www.loc.gov/item/ar0041).
- 1923–24: Woodmen of Union Building (W.T. Bailey design 1922) with bath hotel, hospital, nurse school, bank, theater, print shop; bought by the National Baptist Convention 1948 per NPS — **[DISPUTED]**: Wikipedia names the buyer the National Baptist Association and the year 1950; closed 1983 (conflict: 1981) — see downtown-and-landmarks.md.
- James Truman (Arlington, 1922) and Estella Hughey (Majestic, 1926) were among the first Black masseurs and masseuses — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- 1965: bathhouses desegregated — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). The National Baptist Hotel's manager said of its 1983 closing, "It's a small price to pay for integration" — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm).
- Handling note: slavery and segregation were part of the resort economy; depict factually and carefully (history.md Inferences).

## Game hooks

All mechanics below are **[inferred]** original design ideas drawn from the notes' hook sections; facts they rest on are cited above.
- **Property-trading board game (Bathhouse Barons):** federal Row squares can't be bought, only leased (bid for 20-year leases); an "1877 Commission" card converts squatter tokens into deeds or compensation.
