# Culture, Events and Food in Hot Springs

This file covers the year-round calendar of events in Hot Springs, Arkansas. That includes Oaklawn's January–May race meet, the World's Shortest St. Patrick's Day Parade, the Running of the Tubs, the Documentary Film Festival, Garvan Gardens' tulips and lights, Magic Springs' season, eagle-watch tours and historic spring training.
It also covers the town's signature food and drink: McClard's goat-born barbecue, Mountain Valley water and thermal-water beer brewed in a 1916 bathhouse.
Further sections cover arts, music, film and radio (KTHS, 1924); folklore, ghost stories and "billed as" superlatives; everyday life in each era; and religious and community life.
Dates come from the year named in each source. Most annual events shift by a few days each year.

Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

### World's Shortest St. Patrick's Day Parade (`modern`)
- **Route:** Bridge Street, about 98 ft (98 ft 11 in per Visit Hot Springs). Ripley's called it the "World's Shortest Street in Everyday Use" in the 1940s. Bridge Street calls itself the "world's shortest working street" **[promo]** — [Las Vegas Sun/AP 2025](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2025/mar/17/arkansas-city-boasts-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-d/); [hotsprings.org [promo]](https://www.hotsprings.org/blog/worlds-shortest-st-patricks-day-making-waves-in-a-sea-of-green/).
- **Origin:** Steve Arrison of Visit Hot Springs conceived it over drinks with friends. It debuted on St. Patrick's Day 2004 and took the title from Maryville, Missouri's 99.9-ft parade. Adamsville, R.I. now rivals it with an 89-ft parade. conflict: one source dates the idea to 2003 **[DISPUTED]** — [Visit Hot Springs](https://hotsprings.org/blog/history-of-the-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-day-parade/); [Garden & Gun](https://gardenandgun.com/worlds-shortest-st-patricks-day-parade); [AP via Las Vegas Sun, Mar 2025](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2025/mar/17/arkansas-city-boasts-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-d/).
- **Grand marshals:** the first were city director Robert "Bob" Wheeler and Dick Kelley (Clinton's stepfather). Later marshals included George Wendt, Mike Rowe and Kevin Bacon; Justin Moore (2023); and Valerie Bertinelli (2025, with Flavor Flav as official starter) — [Visit Hot Springs](https://hotsprings.org/blog/history-of-the-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-day-parade/); [Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2025/mar/17/arkansas-city-boasts-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-d/).
- **2026 (23rd annual):** March 17, 2026, at 6:30 p.m. Carmen Electra was grand marshal, riding with the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders. Matt Iseman was official starter, and Vincent Chapman, the Savannah Bananas' "Dancing Umpire," was a guest. The parade also featured a "World's Tallest Leprechaun" — [Visit Hot Springs press release](https://hotsprings.org/media-room/press-releases/celebrities-floats-big-leprechaun-marchers-concert-set-for-first-ever-23rd-annual-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-day-parade-in/); [IrishCentral](https://www.irishcentral.com/events/carmen-electra-grand-marshal-23rd-annual-worlds-shortest-st-patricks-day-parade); [shorteststpats.com](https://shorteststpats.com/2026/02/deputy-assistant-worlds-tallest-leprechaun-takes-over-the-symbolic-shillelagh-for-first-ever-23rd-annual-shortest-st-patricks-day-parade).

### Stueart Pennington World Championship Running of the Tubs (`modern`)
- **Start year: 2006.** Visit Hot Springs billed the 2026 races as the 20th running "since the races began in 2006" — [Visit Hot Springs 2026 Running of the Tubs release](https://hotsprings.org/media-room/press-releases/first-ever-running-of-the-tubs-website-provides-registration-fun-and-background-information-for-2026-world-championship-bathtub/). This agrees with "2018 was the 13th annual" below. The corpus earlier said "created in 2005", citing Arkansas Tourism; the trivia game's source audit found that page gives no year (`games/trivia/research/question-audit.md`, hs-0379), so 2006 is the better-sourced value. The trivia bank's hs-0379 now uses 2006.
- Created to honor Bathhouse Row. Teams push decorated bathtubs on wheels down Central Avenue while spectators throw water balloons and spray water guns. There are cast-iron and modified (plastic or fiberglass) divisions, with obstacles set by judges. Police and fire teams open with a "Battle of the Badges." Winners receive the Steuart Pennington Cup (conflict: spelling Stueart vs Steuart) — [Visit Hot Springs](https://hotsprings.org/blog/running-of-the-tubs-history-lives-in-hot-springs-national-park-arkansas/); [Arkansas Tourism](https://www.arkansas.com/articles/world-championship-running-tubs-hot-springs); [Sentinel-Record](https://www.hotsr.com/news/2016/jun/05/battle-of-the-badges-and-more-entertain/).
- Stueart Pennington was a downtown businessman who wanted bathtub races on Bathhouse Row — [Visit Hot Springs](https://hotsprings.org/blog/running-of-the-tubs-history-lives-in-hot-springs-national-park-arkansas/).
- 2018 was the 13th annual and the first billed as a "World Championship." The 2019 race was canceled, and 2020 moved from May to July because of COVID-19 — [Visit Hot Springs](https://hotsprings.org/blog/running-of-the-tubs-history-lives-in-hot-springs-national-park-arkansas/); [Visit Hot Springs 2020 release](https://hotsprings.org/media-room/press-releases/2020-version-world-championship-running-tubs-batht/).

### Valley of the Vapors Independent Music Festival (`modern`)
- Started March 2005 per [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). conflict: the people notes did not find a founding year. Held in March (March 20–23 in 2020), with 140+ musicians on small stages playing garage, psych rock and synth pop — [Visit Hot Springs guide](https://hotsprings.org/blog/dont-miss-a-reason-to-visit-for-every-season/). ### Labor Day jazz and blues; Hot Springs Rally; Spa-Con; Hot Water Hills (`modern`)

## Food and drink

### McClard's Bar-B-Q (`roaring`→`modern`)
- Alex and Gladys McClard ran the Westside Tourist Court in the 1920s. In 1928 a traveler who couldn't pay $10 for a two-month stay gave them a recipe for "the world's greatest bar-b-que sauce" instead. By 1928 their goat barbecue was so popular that they renamed the court Westside Bar-B-Que. conflict: one article names the wife Alice **[DISPUTED]** — [EOA, McClard's](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mcclards-bar-b-q-4230/); [Visit Hot Springs](https://www.hotsprings.org/blog/mcclards-history-lives-in-hot-springs-national-park-arkansas/).
- It originally smoked goats, not hogs; goat later left the menu, replaced by beef and pork. In 1942 it moved to its current whitewashed stucco building at 505 Albert Pike, which became McClard's. The family later sold it to restaurateur Lee Beasley — [EOA, McClard's](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mcclards-bar-b-q-4230/).
- Bill Clinton is a famous patron who requested McClard's barbecue on Air Force One — [Visit Hot Springs](https://www.hotsprings.org/blog/mcclards-history-lives-in-hot-springs-national-park-arkansas/); [The Takeout](https://thetakeout.com/1696751/mcclard-bbq-arkansas-bill-clinton). 2026 operating status not verified.

### Mountain Valley Spring Water (`frontier`→`modern`)
- Pharmacist Peter E. Greene and his brother John began selling it in 1871 as "Lockett's Spring Water" and renamed it after a nearby community. Peter set up the first distributorship at 195 Central Ave. W. N. Benton, a former St. Louis insurance agent, bought it in 1879. The EOA Central Avenue entry says continuous bottling began in 1871 — [Mountain Valley history](https://www.mountainvalleyspring.com/pages/history); [EoA: Mountain Valley Spring Water](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/mountain-valley-spring-water-2148/); [EOA Central Avenue HD](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-central-avenue-historic-district-7954/); [EOA via search](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=24203).
- EOA says thirteen U.S. presidents drank it, as did Elvis Presley. The company says it reached the White House and Senate by 1920, names presidents from Coolidge to Eisenhower to Clinton, and says it has been served in the U.S. Senate since the 1920s. **[UNVERIFIED]**: no source lists all thirteen presidents — [Mountain Valley history](https://www.mountainvalleyspring.com/pages/history); [The Nibble](https://thenibble.com/reviews/main/beverages/waters/mountain-valley-spring.asp).
- The water comes from a **cold** spring about a dozen miles north of downtown. The headquarters and visitor museum are on Central Avenue — [NWA Democrat-Gazette 2021](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2021/aug/03/high-water-mark/). A renovation of the historic headquarters was completed March 12, 1988 — [EoA This Day Mar 12, 1988](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/this-day-ar-history/this-day-2730/). In the 1913 fire the Rock Island station was reportedly saved with Mountain Valley water — [ADG 2022](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2022/apr/10/hot-springs-battles-with-blazes/).
- Other bottlers: Chewaukla Mineral Springs Co. was incorporated Feb. 8, 1938 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). Lake Ouachita State Park preserves the Three Sisters Springs, once promoted for healing mineral water — [Wikipedia: Lake Ouachita State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ouachita_State_Park).

### Superior Bathhouse Brewery (`modern`)
- In the 1916 Superior Bathhouse. Described as the only brewery in a U.S. national park and the only one brewing with 100% thermal spring water — [Arkansas Tourism](https://www.arkansas.com/hot-springs/landmarks/superior-bathhouse-brewery); [VinePair](https://vinepair.com/articles/arkansas-superior-bathhouse-beer-national-park/). **[DISPUTED/soft claim]**: the thermal-water "only" claim rests partly on owner-brewer Rose Schweikhart's belief — [All About Beer](https://allaboutbeer.com/superior-bathhouse-brewery/).
- NPS pipes the water to the brewery at about 143–144°F, which saves energy because brewing starts by heating water to 160°F. Most hot-spring water elsewhere is not potable (sulfur, bacteria, algae) — [All About Beer](https://allaboutbeer.com/superior-bathhouse-brewery/).
- Opening year: conflict: 2012 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); some sources say 2013 (unverified). The Superior stopped bathing in 1983 (EOA, with furnishings auctioned) or 1984 (NPS) — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/). The name is "Superior Bathhouse Brewery and Distillery" — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).

## Arts, music and film

### Venues and performers
- **The Vapors** (315 Park Ave.) was built in summer 1959 by Dane Harris with Madden and Harry Hastings, and opened in 1960. It had a coffee shop, the Monte Carlo Room, a theater restaurant with a retractable stage and a late-night casino. It also had Tiffany chandeliers and a mahogany bar. Acts included Edgar Bergen, the Smothers Brothers, Tony Bennett, Liberace, Patti Page, Les Paul, Phyllis Diller and Mickey Rooney — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/); [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/); [America's Best Racing](https://www.americasbestracing.net/lifestyle/2020-the-vapors-explores-complicated-history-illegal-gambling-hot-springs); [NWA / Rex Nelson](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2020/jul/12/the-vapors/) (snippet).
- **Tony Bennett** first sang "I Left My Heart in San Francisco" at the Vapors, in rehearsal — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/). The history notes' hook frames it as "to a single bartender" (framing only, **[UNVERIFIED]**).
- Vapors later life: in 1977 it added the Cockeyed Cowboy and the Apollo Disco. It was sold to Tower of Strength Ministries in Oct. 1998, listed for sale in Nov. 2013 and bought in 2017 to become the venue "Vapors Live." David Hill's book *The Vapors* (2020) is the main narrative account — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/); [America's Best Racing](https://www.americasbestracing.net/lifestyle/2020-the-vapors-explores-complicated-history-illegal-gambling-hot-springs).
- **Woodmen of Union Building** (designed 1922 by W.T. Bailey, built 1923–24, 501 Malvern Ave.) hosted Count Basie, Peg Leg Bates and Joe Louis and closed in 1935. Its auditorium figures conflict: 2,500 seats (Preserve Arkansas, Wikipedia) vs a 2,000-seat theater plus a 600-capacity auditorium (NPS) vs 2,000 (Bathhouse Row article) — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [Preserve Arkansas (via search summary)](https://preservearkansas.org/woodmen-of-union-building-national-baptist-hotel/); [Wikipedia: Woodmen of Union Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodmen_of_Union_Building).
- **Arlington Hotel (1924)** amenities included an Arthur Murray dance studio, roof gardens, a 10-piece Meyer-Davis orchestra and the Fountain Room. The Arlington is the town's cultural hub across eras: KTHS (1920s), Capone's suite (1930s), Clinton's prom, film shoots (2018–2022) and the 2025 film festival — [Arlington Hotel history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/).
- **Maxine's:** Maxine Temple Jones's former brothel was renovated in 1989 and reopened in 1991 as Maxine's Coffee House and Puzzle Bar. It is now the live-music venue Maxine's Live — [EOA, Maxine Temple Jones](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/maxine-temple-jones-5338/); [Roadtrippers guide](https://maps.roadtrippers.com/trips/23843966).
- **Ohio Club** offers live jazz and blues and is described as open since 1905 — [Roadtrippers guide](https://maps.roadtrippers.com/trips/23843966). It was gutted by fire in July 2001 — [Las Vegas Sun 2001](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2001/jul/12/historic-gambling-spot-gutted-by-fire/).
- **Timberwood Amphitheater** (Magic Springs, 2003, 5,000 seats) hosts Saturday concerts in season. The 2026 series named Daughtry, Halestorm and Crowder; no list of earlier performers was found — [Wikipedia: Magic Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Springs); [Sentinel-Record, May 11, 2026](https://www.hotsr.com/news/2026/may/11/magic-springs-theme-and-water-park-touts-concerts/).
- **Ozark Bathhouse** housed the Museum of Contemporary Art of Hot Springs from 2009 to 2013. It reopened in 2014 as the Hot Springs National Park Cultural Center, which shows Artist-in-Residence work — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).

"Music Room" is the current NPS name: in 1915 this glass-vaulted room was the assembly or palm room, and the "music room" was the ladies' parlor at its south end (concert grand piano, Victrola) — [NPS Historic Furnishings Report](http://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/hfr-fordyce-batthouse.pdf). Caption it "assembly room (today's Music Room)".

The Oaklawn façade mural (1992, 240 × 14 ft) is billed as the largest hand-crafted artwork at any U.S. thoroughbred track — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/). ## Radio

### KTHS (`roaring`)
- Built in 1924 on the upper floor of the new Arlington Hotel. Test broadcasts began Dec. 11, 1924. The Department of Commerce authorized a Class B station on Dec. 19, 1924, and the first regular broadcast went out on Dec. 20, 1924, at 8:30 p.m. from the ballroom — [EOA, KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/); [Arkansas Democrat-Gazette 2008](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2008/mar/09/remembering-arkansas-hot-springs-kths-was-20080309/).
- conflict: the call letters stand for "Kum To Hot Springs" (newspaper) or "Kome (come) To Hot Springs" (EOA) **[DISPUTED]** — same sources.
- Programming was mostly live big-band music from the hotel ballrooms. Campbell Arnoux was founding director until 1934. The Hot Springs Chamber of Commerce took over on May 27, 1928. The station launched the comedy team Lum and Abner — [EOA, KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/).
- conflict: the early frequency was 600 kHz (blog) or 800 kHz (EOA) **[DISPUTED]**. The station later moved to 1090 kHz and became KAAY — [KAAY history blog](https://kaay1090.blogspot.com/2006/04/kaay-history.html); [Ten Watts blog](https://tenwatts.blogspot.com/2019/10/untangling-kths-am-and-kths-fm.html).
- conflict on the start year: the Arlington history says KTHS, the city's first radio station, broadcast from the hotel in 1928 (the same passage as Joe T. Robinson's nomination), while EOA gives December 1924 — [Arlington Hotel history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/); [EOA, KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/). Use 1924 for the first broadcast; 1928 is the Chamber takeover year.

## Everyday life by era

Period dress, slang and social-club names were **not found** in any notes file. The villager archetypes below are **[inferred]** game roles built from cited jobs, and the characters themselves are invented.

### ancient (<1804)
- **Jobs:** novaculite quarrying and trade ("Arkansas's oldest industry"). Pits were 15–40 ft deep, 10–30 ft wide and 100–300 ft long, and flakes covered hundreds of square miles. The stone was traded over much of North America. Later, French trappers used novaculite whetstones to sharpen skinning knives — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- **Life:** people have lived here more than 10,000 years. The park has 92 archaeological sites (novaculite points, grinding stones). Caddo confederations held the region from about AD 800, and occupation lasted to about AD 1600. Mounds were built over burned buildings in the 1300s–1400s — [NPS Archeology](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/archeology.htm); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- **Food/wildlife:** deer, raccoons, swans and buffalo sign, as noted in 1804 — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).
- **Sensitive context:** European disease pushed Caddo survivors south. Later visitors were displaced individuals rather than intact tribes — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
- Archetypes [inferred]: quarry worker, knapper, trader, storyteller (who tells the neutral-ground legend as legend).

### frontier (1804–1874)
- **Jobs:** renting cabins to health seekers (Percival), hotel keeping (first "hotel" 1825; Belding 1828), trapping (Cates, Percival), enslaved hotel labor, and novaculite whetstone mining (from 1818) — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Arkansas Archeological Survey PDF](https://archeology.uark.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ArkansasNovaculite-rev_acc.pdf).
- **Routine and buildings:** in 1804 there was an empty cabin and huts built by health seekers; log cabins and a store were added for visitors in the 1830s. In 1851 there were two rows of hotels and bathhouses, and by 1873 six bathhouses and 24 hotels and boardinghouses — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/); [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Transport:** river boat, wagon, horseback, then stagecoach. The 21-mile stage from Malvern took most of a day and risked breakdowns, floods and robbers — [EOA Hot Springs Railroad](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157); [NPS Getting to the American Spa](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/kidsyouth/getting-to-hot-springs-national-park.htm).
- **Prices:** New Madrid certificate $260, then $640, then a half interest for $600 (1819). The Louisiana Purchase cost about 3¢ an acre. No board or cabin prices were found — [EOA Rector](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- **Population:** 84 in the township (1830), 201 (1860), 1,276 (1870) — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Drink:** Mountain Valley bottling from 1871 — [Mountain Valley history](https://www.mountainvalleyspring.com/pages/history).
- **Sensitive context:** in 1860, 613 of 5,635 Hot Spring County residents were enslaved, and one hotel advertised baths for enslaved people. In the Civil War nearly the whole population fled to Texas and Louisiana, and guerrillas burned the near-empty town after Sept. 1863 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Archetypes [inferred]: cabin-renting innkeeper, trapper, squatter with a disputed title, pharmacist-turned-water-bottler.

### gilded-spa (1875–1920)
- **Jobs:** bath attendants (mentioned from 1875; mostly African American until the 1980s), masseurs (from the 1890s), "mercury rubbers" (mercury ointment for syphilis), porters and maids, licensed bath physicians, hotel staff, railroad crews, bookmakers, spring-training ballplayers, gator farmers (from 1902), ostrich handlers, photographers, jockeys and trainers — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- **A 1920 bather's routine:** take a "course" of 21 baths prescribed by a federally registered physician, tip an attendant within the 20¢ cap, and stay in a $7/week boarding house. The Majestic shuttled guests to the bathhouses every five minutes — [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm); [Abandoned Arkansas (via search summary)](https://abandonedar.com/the-majestic-hotel/).
- **Prices:** in 1914 the attendant fee was 15¢ per bath or $3 per course. In 1920 a single bath cost $0.50–$0.80 ($0.70–$1.00 with attendant) and a 21-bath course $9–$15 ($13–$19 with attendant), with attendant fees capped at 20¢ or $4 per course. Board started at $7/week, cottages and apartments at $20/month and physicians at $25/month. Free Bathhouse applicants swore a pauper's oath; a false oath carried a fine up to $25, 30 days in jail, or both. 87,040 free baths were given in 1919 — [NPS 1914 annual report (via search summary)](https://npshistory.com/publications/annual_reports/hosp/1914.htm); [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- **Rules:** train "drummers" soliciting patients were illegal. The City Council barred them in 1899, but prosecution took about 30 more years — [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm); [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Entertainment:** Happy Hollow joke photos (1888–1940s), ostrich races (from 1900), the Alligator Farm (1902), Whittington Park merry-go-rounds, roller coasters and vaudeville, Oaklawn (1905; State Fair 1906–1914), spring training (from 1886), the Ohio Club (1905) and Sportsman's/Essex Park racing — [EoA: Whittington Park Historic District](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Whittington-Park-Historic-District-8545) (snippet); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/).
- **Transport:** narrow-gauge rail (1876), standard gauge with Pullmans (Oct. 16, 1889), and three railroads by 1920 with daily through sleepers from Chicago and Memphis. Horsecars ran 1874–1887; electric cars replaced mule cars by 1893 (city marker) or 1895 (Wikipedia). In 1920 autos were allowed on the reservation mountain roads 7 a.m.–6:30 p.m. — [EOA HSRR](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157); [NPS 1920](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm); [Wikipedia: Hot Springs Intracity Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Intracity_Transit); [HMdb marker](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=211154).
- **Farm and rural life:** rocky soil meant small farms of wheat, corn and cattle. A water-powered corn mill ran on the South Fork of the Saline, and charcoal works, stave mills and shingle mills operated nearby (shingle roofs were outlawed after 1913). Timber and tourism boom towns included Bear, Blakely and White Sulphur Springs; Bear started on unfounded rumors of precious metals — [EOA Whittington (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/whittington-garland-county-10724/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- **Hardships:** fires (1878, 1905, 1913), and the 1895 smallpox outbreak and blizzard — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- **Sensitive context:** 1880s Black bathers were barred from the 10 a.m.–noon "best" hours. The Free Bathhouse was segregated in 1898. The Crystal (1904) and Pythian (1914, half-price baths for lodge members) served Black bathers. Lynchings occurred in 1913 and 1922 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/).
- Archetypes [inferred]: bath attendant, Free Bathhouse health seeker, Army-Navy Hospital nurse, White Stockings ballplayer, whetstone cutter, gator farmer, Oaklawn jockey.

### roaring (1921–1946)
- **Jobs:** casino croupiers, bookmakers and wire-room staff, Black masseurs and masseuses (James Truman at the Arlington from 1922, Estella Hughey at the Majestic from 1926), radio announcers and big-band musicians (KTHS, 1924), Dierks timber workers, novaculite miners (1920s–30s), the CCC builders of Lake Catherine State Park (1935), aluminum workers (Reynolds, 1942) and hospital staff — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [EOA, KTHS](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/kths-radio-station-6423/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [Wikipedia: Lake Catherine State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Catherine_State_Park).
- **Routine:** Mayor McLaughlin drove his horse-drawn sulky down Central daily. Gamblers paid fines that worked as licenses, and city employees handed out pink-slip ballots — [EOA McLaughlin](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/leo-patrick-mclaughlin-1712/).
- **Prices:** the Medical Arts Building cost $375,000 (1929). In 1937 the Park Hotel was $3 a day and the Arlington $4.50 a day. The first Arkansas Derby purse was $5,000 (1936). In 1936 bath physicians paid $5–$10 exam fees and attendant caps stayed at 20¢/$4 — [ADG 2024](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2024/may/26/hot-springs-rebound/); [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/); [NPS 1936 brochure (via search summary)](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/brochures/1936/hosp/sec3.htm).
- **Entertainment:** KTHS big bands; the Arlington's Arthur Murray studio and Meyer-Davis orchestra; the Ohio "Cigar Store" during Prohibition; the Woodmen of Union hall (Count Basie, Joe Louis); Oaklawn's reopening (March 1, 1934, about 8,000 fans); Negro league camps (Homestead Grays 1930–31; Pittsburgh Crawfords 1932–35); the Ray Doan school; McClard's goat barbecue (1928); Lake Hamilton boating (dam 1931) — [Arlington Hotel history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Transport:** buses replaced streetcars in 1938 — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs Intracity Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Intracity_Transit).
- **War town:** from August 1944 the Army took over most hotels for a redistribution center that processed 32,000+ (closed Dec. 1945; the Arlington says "until the end of 1946" — conflict). The Arlington Fountain Room became the officers' mess and the ballroom a movie theater. Bathing peaked in 1946 at 1M+ baths (EOA); Wikipedia's Bathhouse Row record is 649,270 baths on the Row — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); [Arlington Hotel history](https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/); [EOA Bathhouse Row](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/bathhouse-row-437/); [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Sensitive context:** the 1941 Mitchell v. United States rail-car case; an anti-gambling ex-sheriff murdered in 1937 — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Archetypes [inferred]: exiled big-city gangster (Madden type), the postmaster's daughter who marries him, croupier, bookmaker, tourist-court owner with a secret sauce, returning GI reformer.

### midcentury (1947–1967)
- **Jobs:** motor-court and motel staff (a 1950–1965 hotel and motel boom tied to casino gambling), casino and Vapors show staff, factory work (Alliance Rubber 1954, Norris Dispensers 1957, Lake Catherine Footwear 1960), animal trainers (IQ Zoo 1955), potters (Dryden from 1956), minor-league Bathers players, rehabilitation-center staff (from 1960), and State Police troopers in the 1967 raids — [Rex Nelson, "Along Park Avenue"](https://www.rexnelsonsouthernfried.com/post/along-park-avenue); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- **Entertainment:** the Vapors (1960) headliners; Belvedere and Southern clubs; IQ Zoo tightrope chickens and fortune-card dispensers; the Miss Arkansas Pageant (from 1958); Bathers baseball (Class C, to 1955); the Hank Aaron (1952) and Jackie Robinson (1953) games; Butchie's Drive-In, Opal's Steak House and the Park Avenue motor courts — [EOA Vapors](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/vapors-3998/); [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2008/09/animals); [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas).
- **Travel:** Chicago & Southern air service from 1948 (merged into Delta 1953); the last passenger train in 1964 — [City of Hot Springs: Airport History](https://www.hotspringsar.gov/476/Airport-History); [EOA HSRR](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-railroad-14157).
- **Decline of bathing:** the Free Bathhouse ended service to indigents in 1956. The Fordyce closed on June 29, 1962 — [NPS African Americans & the Baths](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/african-americans-and-the-hot-springs-baths.htm); [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Sensitive context:** school desegregation began in 1956 with a high-school auto-mechanics course, and bathhouses desegregated in 1965. Lake Ouachita flooded Buckville and Cedar Glades. Bombings: Belvedere (March 11, 1959) and the Vapors (Jan. 4, 1963) — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/); [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/); [ADG 2019](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/).
- Archetypes [inferred]: behaviorist animal trainer, a madam who later writes a memoir (Maxine type), a Hot Springs High graduate headed for politics (adult version of the Clinton type only; never a playable Clinton), a state trooper in 1967.

### revival (1968–1999)
- **Life:** the "empty Row": Maurice 1974, Ozark 1977, Hale 1978, Superior 1983/84, Quapaw 1984, Lamar 1985. Hot Springs Village (1970) brought retirees. New attractions and institutions: Magic Springs (1978), Mid-America Science Museum (1979), the Mountain Tower (1983), Garland County Community College (1973), ASMSA (1993), National Park Medical Center (1985). Festivals began: Documentary Film Festival (1992), Music Festival (1995/96). The Fordyce museum opened in 1989.
