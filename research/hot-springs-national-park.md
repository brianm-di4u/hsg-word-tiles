# Hot Springs National Park: Reservation to Park

How a federally reserved patch of steaming springs became the oldest unit now in the National Park System: the 1818 Quapaw cession, the April 20, 1832 reservation, a roughly 45-year land-claim fight ending in the 1870s, the 1877 commission and superintendents, federal regulation of baths, the March 4, 1921 park designation, and later milestones to 2026.
It also separates verified superlatives from tourism claims. Building-by-building detail lives in bathhouse-row-and-springs.md; spring science (count, temperature, flow, age, geology) in geography-lakes-mountains.md; the chronological backbone in history-timeline.md.
Sources: NPS (nps.gov/hosp, npshistory.com), Encyclopedia of Arkansas (EOA), Wikipedia; "(via search summary)" / `(snippet)` = less verified.

Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

## Land claims and court cases

- **Background (New Madrid certificates).** Congress created New Madrid Certificates in 1815 after the 1811–12 earthquakes. Joseph Story bought Certificate 467 for $260 and sold it to Samuel Hammond for $640 on Jan. 4, 1819; on Feb. 19, 1819 Hammond sold half to Elias Rector for $600. James T. Conway surveyed the hot-springs land in 1819. Elias Rector died August 1822 (in Louisville) — [EOA Rector v. United States](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/).
- **Claimants.** Henry M. Rector (b. 1816, came to Arkansas 1835; later Civil War governor and, per NPS, a bathhouse owner) — [EOA Rector v. US](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/); [NPS People](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm). The Belding family (at the springs no later than 1828; the 1830 Preemption Act covered those farming in 1829) — [EOA Rector v. US](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/). Hale, Belding, Percival and Rector fought ownership in court for almost 25 years — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/). (A Belding-Gaines Cemetery on US 270 E is NRHP-listed — [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas).)

## Park facts and figures

- **Area (conflict):** 2,529.1 acres reserved 1832 — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/); reservation 264.93 acres (1876 context) — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas); 911.63 acres in 1920 — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm); today 5,550 acres (Wikipedia infobox) vs 5,839 acres after expansion (Wikipedia history section) — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- **Mountains and units:** Hot Springs, North, West and Sugarloaf mountains plus Whittington Lake Park — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park). Highest point Music Mountain, 1,405 ft (NPS) vs 1,418 ft (SummitPost) — [NPS: Sunset Trail](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/sunset-trail.htm); [SummitPost](https://www.summitpost.org/music-mountain/154210). City around 600 ft, surrounding hills ~600 ft higher — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- **Springs (summary; full detail in geography-lakes-mountains.md):** 47 springs (Britannica; Wikipedia city) vs 43 presumed flowing, 33 collected at a central reservoir (Wikipedia HSNP) vs "about four dozen" (NPS); about 143°F; "over 700,000 gallons per day" (NPS) vs 750,000–950,000 / ~1,000,000 / 850,000+ (other sources); water about 4,400 years old (USGS PP 1044-C) — [NPS Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm); [USGS PP 1044-C abstract](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/geology/publications/pp/1044-C/abstract.htm); [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park). Historic counts: four principal + two lesser springs at 148–150°F (1804); 44 analyzed (1901); 46 springs, 102–147°F (mostly 135–145°F), 851,308 gal/day (1920) — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/); [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm). The water is monitored to U.S. drinking-water standards; Legionella found in some samples (inhalation concern) — [NPS: Experience the Water](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm).
- **NPS FAQ figures** (added 2026-11-01, pinball research): 47 hot springs averaging 143°F; the park "goes through about 700,000 gallons of water per day"; not volcanic (geothermal gradient about 4°F per 300 ft); organisms include ostracods, blue-green algae and nano-bacteria; park area **5,549.1 acres** (adds to the 5,550 vs 5,839 conflict below); **26 miles** of trails — [NPS FAQ (docx)](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/upload/Common-Frequently-Asked-Questions-v3.docx) (undated, about 2020). USGS: 47 spring boxes, 35 monitored; over 600,000 gal/day distributable to a reservoir under Bathhouse Row; circulation depth 2,000–8,000 ft — [USGS: Hydrology of HSNP](https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/hydrology-hot-springs-national-park). NPS "Experience the Water": 7 thermal fountains, 2 cold fountains (Happy Hollow, Whittington), 6 places to touch thermal water; only the Quapaw (ages 14+) and Buckstaff (ages 10+) offer full thermal-water immersion — [NPS: Experience the Water](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm).
- **Affiliated tribes** (added 2026-11-01, pinball research): NPS names the **Quapaw and Caddo** — [NPS FAQ (docx)](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/upload/Common-Frequently-Asked-Questions-v3.docx). Tourism sites add the Cherokee, Choctaw and others without sources; do not use those.
- **Archaeology:** 92 known sites "and counting" (prehistoric novaculite quarries, campsites, cemeteries); novaculite points and grinding stones; methods LiDAR, ground-penetrating radar, test units; consulting tribes: Absentee Shawnee Tribe, Caddo Nation, Osage Nation, Quapaw Tribe — [NPS Archeology](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/archeology.htm).
- **Forest:** oak/hickory on north slopes, pine on south slopes; old growth ~230 acres on North and Hot Springs mountains and 90 acres on Sugarloaf; many trees 130+ years, a few 200+ (shortleaf pine, blackjack oak, white oak) — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- **Trails (summary; table in geography-lakes-mountains.md):** Sunset Trail ~10 mi one way, the longest (West Mountain 2.8 mi, Sugarloaf Mountain 2.6 mi, Stonebridge Road 3.8 mi) — [NPS: Sunset Trail](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/sunset-trail.htm); Grand Promenade 0.5 mi, 3.7%, 619–717 ft, a National Recreation Trail; Short Cut 0.2 mi at 21.1% the steepest; Peak Trail 0.6 mi to the tower; Gulpha Gorge 0.6 mi, blazed pink; Tufa Terrace 0.3 mi paved — [NPS trails page](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/hot-springs-and-north-mountain-trails.htm). 13.27-mile loop around the boundaries — [FKT route](https://fastestknowntime.com/route/hot-springs-national-park-loop-sunset-trail-gulpha-gorge-hot-springs-mountain-and-west). 1920: more than 11 miles of roads and paths; autos allowed on reservation mountain roads 7 a.m.–6:30 p.m. — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- **Campground:** Gulpha Gorge Campground about 2 miles from downtown — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- **Visitation:** in 1904 about 120,000 people visited at least one national park, about 84% of them Hot Springs — [EOA Fire of 1905](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/Hot-Springs-Fire-of-1905-12570). No recent visitor or bath counts found.
- **Bathhouse Row district:** Central Ave. between Reserve and Fountain streets, about 6 acres; 34°30′49″N 93°3′13″W (NRHP point 34.513889, -93.053611) — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row); [NRHP list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Garland_County,_Arkansas). Called the largest remaining collection of 20th-century bathhouses in the US; cultural landscape periods of significance 1885–1897, 1912–1923, 1930–1959 — [NPS Bathhouse Row Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm).
- **Wildlife:** white-tailed deer, wild turkey, squirrel, rabbit, opossum, gray fox, coyote, skunk, raccoon, gopher, long-tailed weasel, mink, chipmunk, frogs, nine-banded armadillo; extirpated: plains bison, eastern elk, cougar, Gregory's (red) wolf; Mississippi Flyway migrants — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park). See fish-and-wildlife.md.

## NPS visual and built language

- **Main entrance and balustrade (1890s, Robert Stevens):** two bronze federal eagles and a central hemicycle drinking fountain; bandstand on the balustrade removed 1958; a 2010 view shows "symmetrical hedges and columns" — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row); [NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm).
- **Magnolia Promenade (1890s):** originally double rows of magnolias, now a single row; a 1940s caption describes a "tree-lined walkway"; an "informal Victorian landscape design" dates to the 1890s — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row); [NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm).
- **Jens Jensen (1916):** lights and flower gardens at Mather's request — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Grand Promenade:** north–south brick path on the hillside behind the bathhouses, Reserve Ave. to Fountain St.; begun 1930s (grading 1933), gravel by WWII, completed 1958 (NPS; final landscaping July 25, 1958 per the [NPS Grand Promenade sheet](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/bathhouses/grand-promenade.pdf)) / early 1960s (Wikipedia; [NHL nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/74000275_text)); paving brick replaced 1984; replaced a "meandering Victorian path" with a more formal line — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row); [NPS Cultural Landscape](https://www.nps.gov/articles/500341.htm).
- **Rustic trail shelters (1924):** first in May 1924 on the ridge between North and Hot Springs mountains, four more followed; native stone and logs topped with a redwood shingle roof; three survive — [HMdb marker](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=234366). (CCC rustic stone-and-wood work is documented at Lake Catherine State Park, not confirmed in the national park — [Living New Deal](https://livingnewdeal.org/us/ar/hot-springs-ar).)
- **Administration Building (1936; "Built in 1938" per the HABS AR-28 data page — conflict):** faces **Reserve Ave**, not Central (HABS: "the only structure not facing Central Avenue"), at 101 Reserve St — [HABS data pages](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ar/ar0000/ar0031/data/ar0031data.pdf); see `bathhouse-row-layout.md`. NPS Eastern Division architects; Spanish Colonial Revival; clay-tile hip roof, wrought-iron balconies and grilles, a broken arched pediment over a second-story window; cast-concrete fountain in front (1936); jug fountain on the sidewalk (1966); AC replaced 1960, first floor remodeled 1966 — [Wikipedia: Bathhouse Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathhouse_Row).
- **Thermal features:** Display Spring behind the Maurice — a shallow pool "shaded by trees, surrounded by mosses and blue-green algae"; tufa locally "a more drab gray"; an older account describes "brilliant emerald-green tufa" from hot-water algae; blue-green alga *Phormidium treleasei* — [NPS Experience the Water](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm); [NPS What is tufa?](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/photosmultimedia/tufa.htm); [Haywood & Weed](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/hosp/haywood-weed/sec9.htm); [EoA photo entry](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/photo-of-day/this-day-1638/).
- **Towers on Hot Springs Mountain:** Enoch Woolman's wooden tower (1877; ~75 ft per Wikipedia or ~80 ft per Sentinel-Record; lightning in the 1890s) → steel Rix Tower (~165 ft, elevator, telescope; opened 1906 per Sentinel-Record; Wikipedia says relocated 1904 World's Fair wireless tower; razed July 13, 1971 per NPS or 1975 per Wikipedia) → 216-ft lattice steel tower (contract March 1982, opened June 1983) — [NPS: Hot Springs Mountain Tower](https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-mountain-tower.htm); [Wikipedia: Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Mountain_Tower); [Sentinel-Record 2019](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-sentinel-record/20190801/281775630770491).
- NPS sign standards (arrowhead logo, routed-wood signs) were **not sourced**; the corpus's "routed-wood" motif is an inference (visual-style-guide.md). No logos are to be reproduced.
- Image references (consult only, do not download): HABS AR-28 Bathhouse Row & Grand Promenade — [LOC item ar0031](https://www.loc.gov/item/ar0031); NHL photo set — [NPGallery 74000275 photos](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/74000275_photos); NPS postcard finding aid — [NPS docx](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/upload/Postcard-Finding-Aid.docx).

## Game hooks

All **[inferred]** design ideas from the notes, built on facts above; rules must be original.
1. **"You can't own this square" (1832).** Federal Row squares can't be bought, only leased; bidding for 20-year leases — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).
2. **Commission card (1877).** Converts squatter tokens into compensation or a deed — [EOA Rector v. US](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/rector-v-united-states-14155/).
3. **Courtroom track:** chancery (1867) → state Supreme Court (1870) → Court of Claims (1874) → U.S. Supreme Court (1875–76); the 90-day filing deadline (May 31, 1870 act) as a timed quest.
4. **Land-claim deck:** New Madrid Certificate 467 changing hands at $260 / $640 / $600.
5. **Superintendent Kelley arrives (Oct. 1877)** and sets rules on bathhouse squares.
6. **Free Bathhouse + pauper's oath** charity/ethics subplot; Ral City encampment — [NPS Ral Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/ral-spring.htm).
7. **Bath economy sim:** 21-bath courses at $9–$15, 20¢ attendant cap, registered physicians; hiring illegal "drummers" gets guests denied baths — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
8. **National Park Day (March 4, 1921):** rangers and park rules arrive; ranger Cary memorial quest (1927) handled soberly.
9. **Tower upgrade path:** ~75–80 ft wood (1877) → ~165 ft Rix (1906) → 216 ft (1983); climbing clears the map ("120 mile view").
10. **Empty Row (1962–1985) → adaptive reuse (1989–2019):** lease auction for museum, spa, brewery, cultural center or hotel; the Maurice ($31.6M, stained-glass fundraising) as endgame.
11. **Flood events:** May 1990 (thermal pumps contaminated, water shut off) and May 2020 (~85 rescued).
12. **Trivia "Fact or Promo":** oldest park unit (fact) vs "smallest national park" (myth) vs "only brewery in a national park" (billed as).
13. **Collectibles:** 2010 quarter; HABS-style facade drawings; archaeology "grinding stones" museum quest (92 sites).
