# Oaklawn and Horse Racing in Hot Springs

Hot Springs has raced horses since Sportsman's Park in the early 1890s; Oaklawn, formed by Southern Club owners in 1904 and opened in 1904 or 1905 (sources conflict), survived betting bans in 1907 and 1919, an unauthorized 1934 reopening under Mayor Leo McLaughlin, and 1935 pari-mutuel legalization. Owned by the Cella family of St. Louis since at least 1914, it runs a one-mile dirt oval whose winter–spring meet builds to the Grade I Arkansas Derby (first run 1936, $5,000 purse). After 1967 it was the town's only legal gambling; it added simulcasting (1989), instant racing (1999), games of skill (2005), a full casino (April 1, 2019, Amendment 100) and a $100 million hotel and event center (completed 2021). ## Track facts

- One-mile dirt oval with a backstretch chute for 6-furlong sprints — [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort).
- Two finish lines: mile races finish at the sixteenth pole, shortening the stretch from 1,155 ft to 825 ft — W-OAK.
- No turf course mentioned — W-OAK.
- 1905 grandstand: glass-enclosed with steam heat, among the first U.S. tracks to have either; 1,500 seats — [EOA-OAK](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=4521).
- Original clubhouse (1904) by Zachary Taylor Davis, later architect of Old Comiskey Park and Wrigley Field — W-OAK.
- 1975: five-level glass enclosure added to grandstand — W-OAK.
- 1992: race times recorded in hundredths of a second — W-OAK.
- 1992: mural 240 ft × 14 ft over the main entrance, billed as the largest hand-crafted artwork at any U.S. thoroughbred track — EOA-OAK; history.md: "a 240×14-ft façade mural in 1992" — [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/).
- American Pharoah bronze (with jockey Victor Espinoza) by sculptor James Peniston, unveiled before the 2018 season — W-OAK.

## Famous horses

- **American Pharoah:** won the Rebel and the Arkansas Derby in 2015, then the first Triple Crown in 37 years. Bronze statue with jockey Victor Espinoza by James Peniston unveiled before the 2018 season — [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- **Smarty Jones:** won the $5 million centennial bonus in 2004 for sweeping the Rebel, Arkansas Derby and Kentucky Derby — W-OAK.
- **Zenyatta:** won the Apple Blossom in 2008 (by 4½ lengths, fourth career start) and on April 9, 2010 (by 4¼ lengths, 16th straight win) — [The Horse](https://thehorse.com/154260/zenyatta-makes-it-16-for-16-in-apple-blossom/); [ABR slideshow](https://www.americasbestracing.net/node/42240) (snippet).

- Others named as Arkansas Derby winners: Sunny's Halo 1983, Tank's Prospect 1985, Pine Bluff 1992, Victory Gallop 1998, Afleet Alex 2005, Curlin 2007, Creator 2016 — [KentuckyDerby.com](https://kentuckyderby.com/?p=21445) (snippet).

## Political and legal history

- 1903 anti-gambling repeal → 1904 Essex Park + Oaklawn Jockey Club → Feb 27, 1907 anti-betting law closes track ~a decade → 1915 Gov. Hays veto of racing bill → March 11, 1916 nonprofit reopening by Business Men's League (unofficial wagering) → 1919 Circuit Judge Scott Wood rules it illegal → 1929 Osro Cobb tie-breaking vote → March 1, 1934 reopening without legislative consent (McLaughlin group, 8,000 fans) → 1935 pari-mutuel law (Gov. J.M. Futrell) — [EOA-OAK](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=4521); [EOA Oaklawn](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/oaklawn-park-racetrack-3636/); [W-OAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaklawn_Racing_Casino_Resort).
